# Category 4: Childhood Fears — Storylines

The universal, self-created terrors every kid experienced alone in the dark.
All use Minecraft blocky voxel style, 9:16, and American English. The detailed beat timings below describe one 30s story. wan3.0 uses one 30s prompt; MiniMax H3 divides the four beats across two separately generated 15s prompts.

## Beat Structure (4 beats)

Unlike Category 1's fantasy-vs-reality split, childhood fears are pure imagination — the fear IS the story:

1. **Setup (0-7s)**: The ordinary situation — a dark hallway, a quiet bedroom, a basement door. Everything is normal. The child is alone.
2. **The Fear Emerges (7-16s)**: The child's imagination transforms the ordinary into the terrifying. Shadows become monsters. Sounds become footsteps. The mundane becomes menacing.
3. **The Escape (16-24s)**: The child's desperate flight — running up stairs, diving under covers, slamming doors. Heart pounding. Breath visible.
4. **Safe (24-30s)**: The child is safe. Under the blanket. Door closed. The fear recedes. But the child knows... it's still out there. A final, lingering glance at the source of fear.

## 4.1 — Running Up the Stairs in the Dark

**Hook**: You turned off the basement light. The stairs are behind you. Something is coming.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A blocky basement. Dim. A single bare pixel bulb hangs from the ceiling. A cube-shaped child has just finished grabbing something from the storage shelf. The child reaches up and pulls the light cord. Click. The basement plunges into darkness. The child stands at the bottom of the stairs, looking up. The stairs stretch upward into a rectangle of faint light from the open door at the top. The child takes a deep breath. Pixel breath visible in the cold air.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child starts walking up the stairs. Normal pace. Then the child's imagination kicks in. The darkness behind the child begins to shift. A pixel shadow — tall, thin, with too many arms — detaches from the wall and begins to climb the stairs. The child's pace quickens. The shadow's pace quickens. The child's footsteps become louder. The shadow's silent footsteps aren't footsteps at all — they're fingertips. Long, pixelated fingertips scraping the wall. The child starts running. The shadow starts running. The stairs seem to multiply — the door at the top isn't getting any closer. The child's pixel breath is now a rapid fog. The shadow's hand reaches out — inches from the child's back.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The child lunges through the doorway, slams the door shut, and fumbles for the light switch. The light flicks on. The child presses their back against the door, chest heaving, pixel sweat droplets on the forehead. The child slowly turns around. Looks at the door. The doorknob. Is it... turning? The child's hand is still on the light switch. The child stares at the doorknob for a long, tense moment. It doesn't move. The child exhales.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child walks away from the door, toward the living room. The child's pace is normal now. Casual. The child glances back at the basement door one more time. It's just a door. The child turns back around. Continues walking. The camera stays on the door. For a beat. Then — the doorknob turns. Just a little. The camera cuts to black. Subtitle: "You know it's still there."

**Music**: 8-bit ambient drone → escalating heartbeat + scraping strings → sudden silence → single ominous piano note

## 4.2 — The Midnight Bathroom Run

**Hook**: 3 AM. You have to pee. The bathroom is at the end of the dark hallway.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A dark blocky bedroom. The clock reads 3:00 AM. A cube-shaped child is in bed, eyes wide open. The child's bladder is sending urgent signals. The child looks at the bedroom door — closed. The child looks at the window — dark. The child looks at the closet — also closed, thank god. The child lifts the blanket. Puts one foot on the cold floor. The floor creaks. The child freezes. Listens. Nothing. The child's pixel breath is visible.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child is now standing in the bedroom doorway, looking down the hallway. The hallway is impossibly long. The bathroom door at the end seems miles away. The hallway is lined with family photos — but in the dark, the faces in the photos look different. Wrong. Watching. The child starts walking. Tiptoeing. Each floorboard creaks at exactly the wrong moment. A shadow shifts at the end of the hallway — was that the cat? The cat is sleeping on the child's bed. The child's pace quickens. The hallway seems to stretch. The bathroom door seems to recede. The child is practically running now. The child's imagination fills the hallway behind them with every monster from every movie they shouldn't have watched.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The child reaches the bathroom. Flicks on the light. The monsters vanish. The child does their business as fast as humanly possible. Washes hands in record time. Looks in the mirror — the reflection is normal. The child turns off the bathroom light. The darkness returns instantly. The child FLINGS the bathroom door open and SPRINTS back down the hallway. The hallway is now ten times longer and ten times darker. The child's feet barely touch the ground. The child dives through the bedroom door, leaps onto the bed — making sure no part of the body touches the gap between the bed and the floor — and pulls the blanket over the head.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child is under the blanket. Breathing hard. Pixel heart icon pounding in the corner of the frame. The child peeks out from under the blanket. The room is normal. The hallway is dark but empty. The child pulls the blanket back over the head. The blanket glows faintly from within — the child has turned on a flashlight. The child is safe. For now. The camera pans to the bedroom door. It's slightly ajar. The hallway beyond is dark. The camera holds on the darkness. Something blinks. Subtitle: "3:00 AM. Every night."

**Music**: 8-bit ticking clock → creeping suspense strings → frantic chase music → soft, relieved exhale → single uneasy note

## 4.3 — The Closet Door

**Hook**: It was closed when you went to sleep. Now it's open. Just a crack.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A dark blocky bedroom. The clock reads 11:30 PM. A cube-shaped child is in bed, eyes closed, breathing steadily. The camera slowly pans across the room — the nightlight, the toy box, the window, the closet. The closet door is closed. The camera lingers on it for a moment. Nothing. The child shifts in bed. The camera returns to the child's face — peaceful. The child rolls over. The camera pans back to the closet. The closet door is now open. Just a crack. About two inches. A sliver of absolute darkness.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child's eyes flutter open. The child looks at the ceiling. Then slowly, reluctantly, turns toward the closet. The child sees the crack. The child's body goes rigid. The child stares at the crack. The crack stares back. The child's imagination floods the darkness with possibilities — a pixel eye blinks in the crack. A pixel hand with too many fingers wraps around the door edge. A pixel face — featureless except for a smile — presses against the opening. The child squeezes eyes shut. Opens them. The crack is just a crack. The child squeezes eyes shut again. Opens them. The crack is wider now. The child's pixel breath is visible. The child is frozen. Can't move. Can't scream. Can't look away.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The child makes a decision. The child throws off the blanket, scrambles out of bed, and runs to the closet. The child SLAMS the closet door shut. The child's hand is on the door, holding it closed. The child's chest is heaving. The child looks around the room — the nightlight is flickering. The child grabs a chair from the desk. Drags it across the room. Wedges it under the closet doorknob. The child steps back. The chair is holding. The child looks at the closet. The closet is silent. The child gets back into bed. Pulls the blanket up. Eyes on the closet.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child is in bed, eyes fixed on the closet. The chair is still there. The door is still closed. The child's eyes are getting heavy. The child's grip on the blanket loosens. The child's breathing steadies. The child falls asleep. The camera holds on the child's peaceful face. Then slowly pans to the closet. The chair is still wedged under the doorknob. The door is still closed. The camera holds. The nightlight flickers once. The chair slides backward. One inch. By itself. The camera cuts to black. Subtitle: "It was already open."

**Music**: 8-bit lullaby → dissonant string swell → sudden silence → heartbeat → soft lullaby returns, but slightly off-key

## 4.4 — The Mirror Game

**Hook**: Everyone at the sleepover said it works. Don't say the name three times.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A blocky bathroom. Dim. A cube-shaped child stands in front of the mirror, holding a pixel flashlight under the chin. The light casts eerie shadows upward across the cube face. The child is at a sleepover. Earlier, the other kids were telling ghost stories. One of them dared the child to say the name three times in the mirror. The child laughed it off. Now it's 2 AM. Everyone else is asleep. And the child is in the bathroom. Alone. With the mirror. The child's reflection stares back. The child's mouth opens. Closes. The child shakes their head. "This is stupid." The child turns to leave. Stops. Turns back to the mirror.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child whispers the name. Once. The bathroom light flickers. The child's eyes go wide. The child whispers the name again. Twice. The mirror fogs up — a patch of pixel condensation forming in the center. The child is gripping the sink. The child's reflection is still there — but it's not moving. The child is frozen in fear, but the reflection is frozen differently — its head is tilted slightly. The child whispers the name a third time. The bathroom light goes out completely. The only light is the flashlight under the child's chin. The mirror is completely fogged now. The child stares at the fogged mirror. A pixel handprint appears on the inside of the glass. Then another. Then another. Something is pressing against the other side.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The child screams — a silent pixel scream, no sound comes out. The child drops the flashlight. The flashlight rolls across the floor, beam spinning wildly, illuminating the bathroom in chaotic flashes. The child scrambles backward, hits the bathroom door, fumbles for the doorknob. The door won't open. The child pulls harder. The mirror is now completely covered in handprints from the inside. The flashlight beam strobes across the mirror — the handprints are moving. The child yanks the door open and bursts into the hallway. The child runs back to the sleeping bags in the living room.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child dives into the sleeping bag, pulls it over the head. The other kids are all asleep. The child's pixel heart is pounding. The child peeks out. The living room is normal. The bathroom door is closed. The child stares at it. Nothing happens. The child slowly lowers the sleeping bag. The child lies on their back, staring at the ceiling. The child's breathing slows. The child closes their eyes. The camera pans to the bathroom door. It's closed. The camera holds. The doorknob turns. The door opens. Just a crack. A sliver of darkness. Subtitle: "It's just a game. Right?"

**Music**: 8-bit innocent music box → discordant electronic noise → strobing panic → dead silence → music box returns, warped and slow

## 4.5 — What's Under the Bed

**Hook**: Your foot is hanging off the edge. Something just touched it.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A dark blocky bedroom. The clock reads 1:00 AM. A cube-shaped child is asleep in bed, one foot dangling off the edge. The room is quiet. The child's breathing is steady. The camera is low, at floor level, looking up at the bed. The space under the bed is a rectangle of absolute darkness. The child's foot twitches in sleep. The child rolls over. The foot is now fully hanging off the edge — toes inches from the floor. The camera holds on the darkness under the bed. Nothing. Then — a pixel hand, pale and long-fingered, slowly rises from the darkness. It reaches toward the child's foot. The fingers are almost touching the heel.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child's eyes snap open. The child feels something. The child looks down at the foot. Nothing there. The child pulls the foot back under the blanket. The child's eyes scan the room. Everything is in its place. The child starts to relax. Then hears a sound. A soft scratching. Coming from under the bed. The child's body goes rigid. The scratching continues — slow, deliberate, like fingernails dragging across the wooden floor. The child pulls the blanket up to the chin. The scratching stops. Silence. The child starts to exhale. Then — a soft breath. Warm. Against the child's hanging foot. The child yanks the foot up.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The child is now curled in the center of the bed, in a tight ball, blanket wrapped around like a cocoon. No body part is touching the edge of the bed. The child's eyes are wide, darting around the room. The scratching resumes — now from the foot of the bed. Then the side. Then the headboard. It's circling. The child is surrounded. The child squeezes eyes shut. The scratching gets louder. Louder. Then stops. Complete silence. The child opens one eye. The room is quiet. The child slowly uncurls. Looks around. Nothing. The child takes a shaky breath. The child's foot, under the blanket, unconsciously shifts toward the edge of the bed. The blanket lifts slightly — the foot is about to hang off again.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child realizes what's happening and yanks the foot back. The child sits up, turns on the bedside lamp. The room floods with warm light. The child looks under the bed. Grabs the flashlight. Shines it. Nothing. Just dust bunnies and a lost sock. The child exhales. Turns off the lamp. Lies back down. But this time, the child is positioned in the exact center of the bed. Feet tucked in. Blanket wrapped tight. No body part exposed. The child closes their eyes. The camera slowly descends to floor level, looking under the bed. The darkness is empty. The camera holds. A pixel hand slowly emerges from the darkness. It reaches up. The camera cuts to black. Subtitle: "You checked. It wasn't there. But it is now."

**Music**: 8-bit gentle sleep music → scratching, scraping, breath sounds → panicked silence → warm light chime → deep, low bass rumble

## Childhood Fears Category — Common Elements

- **The fear is self-created**: The monsters are never shown to be real — the horror is always in the child's imagination, which makes it scarier
- **The ordinary becomes terrifying**: Stairs, hallways, closets, mirrors, under the bed — everyday objects
- **The child is always alone**: No parents, no siblings — solo confrontation with fear
- **The escape is always frantic**: Running, diving, slamming — the child's desperate flight is the most relatable part
- **The lingering threat**: The fear is never fully resolved. The ending always leaves a question — was it really just imagination?
- **No dialogue**: Pure visual horror — the child never speaks, the fear is communicated through body language and sound
- **The ambiguous ending**: The final shot always suggests the fear might be real after all
- **Subtitles**: Creepy, playful, knowing — they acknowledge the shared experience
- **Music**: Starts innocent, builds to terror, ends with an uneasy ambiguity — never fully resolves
- **Sound design is critical**: Scratching, breathing, footsteps, door creaks — the audio does half the horror work
