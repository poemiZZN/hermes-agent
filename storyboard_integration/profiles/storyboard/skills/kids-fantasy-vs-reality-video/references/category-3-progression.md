# Category 3: Skill Progression — First Time → 30th Time → Master Level

The universal journey from terrified beginner to confident expert.
All use Minecraft blocky voxel style, 9:16, and American English. The detailed stage timings below describe one 30s story. wan3.0 uses one 30s prompt; MiniMax H3 divides the progression across two separately generated 15s prompts, each submitted with `duration=15`.

## Beat Structure (3 stages × ~10s)

Each storyline shows the same person doing the same activity at three different skill levels:

1. **First Time (0-10s)**: Fear, tension, shaking hands, wide eyes, eventual failure/crash. The body is stiff, the face is terrified, every movement is uncertain.
2. **30th Time (10-20s)**: Comfortable, doing it properly, relaxed expression, following the rules. The body is loose, the face is neutral or mildly pleased, movements are correct but conventional.
3. **Master Level (20-30s)**: Confident, bold, breaking all the rules, doing tricks, exaggerated expressions. The body is fluid, the face is smug/joyful, movements are flashy and unconventional.

**Visual transition between stages**: A pixel calendar rapidly flipping through dates, or a pixel clock spinning, or a time-lapse of the same location with changing light/weather. A brief subtitle card labels each stage: "1st time" → "30th time" → "Master."

**The core contrast**: Stage 1 vs Stage 3 are complete opposites — same person, same activity, unrecognizable difference. Stage 2 is the bridge that makes the transformation believable.

## 3.1 — Learning to Ride a Bike

**Stage 1: First Time (0-10s)**

A blocky suburban street. A cube-shaped child sits on a bike, training wheels just removed. The child's knuckles are white on the handlebars. The child's face is pure terror — cube eyes wide, mouth a tiny trembling square. A blocky dad holds the seat from behind. The child starts pedaling. The bike wobbles violently — left, right, left — the child's legs pumping frantically, body completely rigid. Dad lets go. The child realizes. Looks back. The bike veers straight into a pixel bush. Child and bike crash in a tangle of limbs and spinning wheels. Pixel tear particles. Pixel birds scatter.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips rapidly — sun rises and sets, leaves change color, rain falls, snow melts.

**Stage 2: 30th Time (10-20s)**

Same street, different season. The same child, slightly taller, gets on the bike with a casual hop. Hands rest lightly on the handlebars. The child starts pedaling smoothly — no wobble, no hesitation. The face is relaxed, a small satisfied smile. The child rides down the street in a straight line, following all the rules — staying in the bike lane, signaling turns with a cube hand, stopping at the stop sign. It's perfectly competent. It's also... a little boring. The child's eyes drift to the neighbor kids doing wheelies.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips even faster — years passing, the child growing taller, the bike getting smaller relative to the child.

**Stage 3: Master Level (20-30s)**

Same street, now. The child — now older, cooler, wearing sunglasses — approaches the bike. Doesn't even look at it. Jumps on from behind in one fluid motion. The bike takes off. The child pops a wheelie, riding on the back wheel alone for several meters. Transitions to no-hands riding — arms spread wide like wings. Then stands on the seat, balancing perfectly. Finishes with a skidding stop that kicks up pixel dust. The child looks at the camera, takes off the sunglasses, and winks. The same pixel bush from Stage 1 is in the background — the child deliberately rides past it and gives it a playful high-five. The bush rustles happily.

**Ending subtitle**: "From scraped knees to no hands."

**Music**: Stage 1 — 8-bit anxious waltz, discordant notes → Stage 2 — steady, pleasant cycling melody → Stage 3 — high-energy rock guitar solo with triumphant finish

## 3.2 — Learning to Cook

**Stage 1: First Time (0-10s)**

A blocky kitchen. A cube-shaped child stands on a stool, facing the stove. A frying pan sits on the burner. An egg is in the child's trembling cube hand. The child's face is a mask of concentration and fear. The child taps the egg on the counter — too hard. The egg explodes, shell and yolk everywhere. Pixel egg particles drip down the cabinet. The child's face is covered in raw egg. The child stares at the mess, frozen, a pixel tear forming. The pan on the stove is still empty. The stove isn't even on.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — seasons change outside the kitchen window.

**Stage 2: 30th Time (10-20s)**

Same kitchen. The same child, slightly older, wearing an apron now. The child cracks an egg with one hand — clean break, perfect separation. Pours it into the hot pan. The egg sizzles. The child adds salt with a precise pinch. Flips the egg with a spatula — no break. Slides it onto a plate. It's a perfect sunny-side-up egg. The child looks at it with quiet satisfaction. The child's mom, in the background, gives a nod of approval. The child eats the egg with a fork. It's fine. It's an egg. The child's expression is... fine. This is fine. It's just an egg.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — the child grows, the kitchen gets slightly different appliances.

**Stage 3: Master Level (20-30s)**

Same kitchen. The child — now a teenager in a chef's hat — stands before the stove like a concert pianist before a grand piano. The child cracks two eggs simultaneously, one in each hand. The pan is already sizzling with butter, onions, and peppers. The child tosses the pan with a flick of the wrist — the omelet flips in the air, does a full rotation, and lands back in the pan. The child adds cheese with a dramatic flourish. Plates the omelet. But that's not all — the child grabs a bottle of ketchup and draws a perfect pixel smiley face on the plate. The child's mom walks in, sees the creation, and her jaw drops. The child hands her a fork. She takes a bite. Her deadpan expression cracks into the biggest smile. The child leans against the counter, arms crossed, smug.

**Ending subtitle**: "From egg on face to chef's kiss."

**Music**: Stage 1 — 8-bit clumsy xylophone, discordant splat → Stage 2 — calm, orderly kitchen waltz → Stage 3 — dramatic cooking show orchestral fanfare with sizzle beats

## 3.3 — Learning to Swim

**Stage 1: First Time (0-10s)**

A blocky public pool. Summer. A cube-shaped child stands at the edge of the pool, wearing inflatable pixel arm floaties and clutching a kickboard. The child's entire body is shaking. The child's cube teeth are chattering — pixel chatter particles. The water looks impossibly deep, impossibly blue, impossibly scary. A swimming instructor in the water beckons with a gentle hand. The child takes one step forward. The feet slip on the wet tile. The child pinwheels arms wildly, then falls into the pool with a massive pixel splash. The child surfaces, sputtering, arm floaties twisted, kickboard floating away. The child's face is a mixture of shock and betrayal. The water is only knee-deep.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — the pool changes from summer crowds to empty autumn to indoor winter pool.

**Stage 2: 30th Time (10-20s)**

Same pool. The same child, now comfortably in a swimsuit without floaties, stands at the edge. The child dives in with a clean, if somewhat cautious, entry. The child swims freestyle across the lane — proper form, steady breathing, turns at the wall with a competent flip turn. The child's face is calm and focused. The child completes the lap, touches the wall, and checks the time on the pool clock. A small nod of satisfaction. The child climbs out, wraps a towel around the shoulders, and sits on a bench. The swimming instructor gives a thumbs up. The child returns it. Everything is by the book. Everything is correct. Everything is... fine.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — seasons change, the child grows taller, the pool gets new lane lines.

**Stage 3: Master Level (20-30s)**

Same pool. The child — now a teenager in sleek competitive swimwear — stands on the diving block. The child adjusts goggles with one hand. The child's body is lean and powerful. The starting buzzer sounds. The child launches off the block in a perfect racing dive — barely a splash. Underwater, the child dolphin-kicks past the entire field. Surfaces halfway down the pool. The child's stroke is effortless, fluid, almost lazy — but unbelievably fast. The child touches the wall, looks at the clock, and doesn't even seem winded. The child climbs out, shakes water from the hair like a shampoo commercial, and walks past the diving board. On impulse, the child runs up, bounces, and executes a perfect cannonball — the biggest pixel splash in pool history. The other swimmers in the lane are soaked. The child surfaces, grinning. The swimming instructor is in the background, holding a stopwatch, jaw on the floor.

**Ending subtitle**: "From floaties to cannonballs."

**Music**: Stage 1 — 8-bit nervous water droplets, plinking fear → Stage 2 — steady, rhythmic swimming metronome → Stage 3 — dramatic orchestral race theme → playful cannonball splash chord

## 3.4 — Learning to Skateboard

**Stage 1: First Time (0-10s)**

A blocky skatepark. A cube-shaped child stands at the top of a very small, very gentle ramp — basically a slight incline. The child is wearing every piece of protective gear ever invented: helmet, elbow pads, knee pads, wrist guards, and what appears to be a pixel pillow strapped to the back. The skateboard is brand new, grip tape still shiny. The child places one foot on the board. The board rolls forward one centimeter. The child's arms pinwheel. The child falls backward immediately — the pixel pillow absorbs the impact. The child lies on the ground, staring at the sky, helmet slightly askew. The skateboard is still rolling. It continues rolling for about two meters, then gently tips over. The child doesn't get up.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — the skatepark changes from empty to crowded to snowy to sunny again.

**Stage 2: 30th Time (10-20s)**

Same skatepark. The same child, no pillow, just a helmet and knee pads now. The child pushes off confidently, rides across the flat ground with a smooth cruising stance. The child ollies over a small crack in the pavement — the board leaves the ground by maybe two inches. It's technically an ollie. The child lands with a slight wobble but stays on. The child rides up a small ramp, comes back down, and rolls to a stop. The child's face is relaxed, focused but not tense. The child checks the knee pads — they're scuffed, well-used. The child nods to a friend who's also skating. They ride together in parallel, not doing anything fancy, just cruising. It's competent. It's smooth. It's... fine.

**Transition**: Pixel calendar flips — the child's knees get progressively more scarred, the skateboard gets progressively more worn.

**Stage 3: Master Level (20-30s)**

Same skatepark. The child — now a teenager, no protective gear at all, wearing ripped jeans and a backwards cap — drops into the biggest ramp in the park without hesitation. The child soars up the opposite wall, grabs the board mid-air, spins it, and lands back on it. The child ollies over a row of three pixel trash cans. Kickflips down a set of stairs — the board flips perfectly under the feet. The child grinds a rail, sparks flying from the pixel trucks. Lands, rides out, and kicks the board up into the hand. The child's friends are going wild in the background. The child looks at the camera, takes off the backwards cap, and runs a hand through the hair. Behind the child, the exact spot of the Stage 1 fall is visible — a tiny pixel dent in the ground. The child glances at it, smiles, and drops the board to ride away.

**Ending subtitle**: "From pillow armor to kickflips."

**Music**: Stage 1 — 8-bit wobbly cartoon fall → Stage 2 — chill lo-fi cruising beat → Stage 3 — aggressive punk rock skate anthem with board-slide scratches

## Progression Category — Common Elements

- **Same person, same place, same activity**: The continuity makes the transformation visible
- **Stage 1 is always failure**: The first attempt must end in a crash, a mess, or a fall
- **Stage 2 is competent but boring**: The middle stage shows skill but no flair — it's correct, not exciting
- **Stage 3 is pure confidence**: The master stage breaks rules, does tricks, and makes it look effortless
- **The environment ages**: The location changes subtly across stages — seasons, wear and tear, the child growing
- **Visual motif**: The pixel calendar or clock transition between stages
- **Ending callback**: Stage 3 always includes a nod to Stage 1's failure (the bush, the egg mess, the shallow water, the dent in the ground)
- **Subtitles**: "From X to Y" format capturing the transformation journey
- **Music**: Three distinct movements — anxious → steady → triumphant — with a callback to the Stage 1 theme in the Stage 3 ending
