# Category 2: Growing Up Stories — Storylines

Childhood memories centered on family: siblings, parents, and the moments that define growing up.
All use Minecraft blocky voxel style, 9:16, and American English. The detailed beat timings below describe one 30s story. wan3.0 uses one 30s prompt; MiniMax H3 divides the four beats across two separately generated 15s prompts.

## Beat Structure (4 beats)

1. **Setup (0-7s)**: Establish the family scenario — two siblings, a parent and child, a family moment
2. **Conflict/Tension (7-16s)**: The conflict emerges — rivalry, misunderstanding, fear, or resistance
3. **Turning Point (16-24s)**: A gesture, a realization, a parent's intervention — the moment things shift
4. **Warm Resolution (24-30s)**: The love beneath the surface becomes visible — a shared moment, a quiet understanding

## 2.1 — The Last Cookie (Sibling Rivalry)

**Hook**: Two siblings. One cookie. Neither will back down.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A blocky kitchen counter. One chocolate chip cookie on a plate. Two cube siblings — brother and sister — stand on opposite sides, eyes locked on the cookie. The camera slowly pushes in. A pixel tumbleweed rolls across the counter. Neither moves.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The sister's hand twitches. The brother's eyes narrow. Close-ups flick between their faces. The sister feints. The brother blocks. Both lunge simultaneously. Cube hands collide. The cookie flies into the air in slow motion — chocolate chips scattering as pixel particles. It lands on the floor and breaks into two halves. One big. One small.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: Silence. Both staring at the broken pieces. The sister reaches down. Picks up both halves. Looks at her brother. Hands him the bigger half. The brother's eyes soften. He takes it, looks at it, then breaks off a piece and hands it back. They sit on the kitchen floor, shoulders touching.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: They eat their cookie pieces in silence. The sister leans her head on the brother's shoulder. He doesn't move away. A pixel heart floats up between them. Subtitle: "Siblings."

**Music**: 8-bit spaghetti western guitar → tense ticking → warm acoustic guitar resolution

## 2.2 — Dad's Secret Scar (Parent-Child Bond)

**Hook**: Dad never talks about the scar on his knee. Today, he shows it.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A blocky living room. A cube-shaped child is sitting on the floor, holding a scraped knee. The child fell off a bike. Pixel tear particles. A blocky dad walks in, kneels down. Looks at the knee. Doesn't say anything. Just sits down next to the child.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child points at the scrape, face full of misery. The dad looks at it. Then the dad slowly rolls up his own pant leg. On his knee, there's a pixel scar — old, faded, but unmistakable. The child's eyes go wide. The dad points at the scar. Points at the child's scrape. Points at the scar again. The child's crying stops. The child reaches out and touches the dad's scar with one cube finger. Very gently.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: The dad doesn't say anything. He just sits there, letting the child touch the scar. The child looks at the dad's face. The dad's expression is somewhere between a smile and a memory. The child looks back at the scar. Back at their own scraped knee. The child's own scrape suddenly seems smaller. The child wipes the tears with the back of a hand. The dad reaches over and ruffles the child's hair.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: The child gets up. The dad gets up. The child walks toward the door — toward the bike that's still lying on the driveway. The child stops at the door. Looks back at the dad. The dad is still standing there, pant leg still rolled up. The dad gives a small nod. The child nods back. The child walks out. The dad watches. Rolls down the pant leg. The scar is hidden again. But now the child knows it's there. Subtitle: "We all have scars."

**Music**: 8-bit sad piano → gentle strings → warm orchestral swell

## 2.3 — Mom's Midnight Snack (Parent-Child Alliance)

**Hook**: Caught sneaking snacks. Mom doesn't get mad. She joins in.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A dark blocky kitchen. A cube-shaped child is standing on a stool, reaching into the highest cabinet for the cookie jar. The child's pajamas are rumpled. The child freezes mid-reach — the kitchen light flicks on. The child slowly turns around. Mom is standing in the doorway, arms crossed, deadpan expression.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child's face cycles through emotions: guilt, fear, a desperate attempt to look innocent. The cookie jar is still in the child's hands. There is no excuse. Mom stares. The child stares back. The child slowly puts the cookie jar down. The child's shoulders slump. The child is about to apologize. Mom holds up a hand. The child freezes.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: Mom walks to the cabinet. The child watches, confused. Mom reaches up — into the same cabinet — and pulls out another jar. Chocolate spread. Mom takes two spoons from the drawer. She sits down at the kitchen table. Opens the cookie jar. Opens the chocolate spread. She looks at the child. Gestures to the chair across from her. The child's jaw drops. The child scrambles into the chair. Mom dips a cookie into the chocolate spread. Hands it to the child. The child takes it. The child's face is pure disbelief.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: They eat cookies together in the dark kitchen. Mom dips. Child eats. Mom dips. Child eats. The only light is from the fridge, which someone left slightly open. Mom's deadpan expression cracks into a tiny smile. The child sees it and grins back, mouth full of cookie. Mom puts a finger to her lips. "Don't tell Dad." The child nods solemnly, mouth still full. A pixel heart floats up between them. Subtitle: "Midnight alliance."

**Music**: 8-bit sneaky bass → record scratch → warm acoustic guitar → soft kitchen lullaby

## 2.4 — The Pillow Fort (Sibling Teamwork)

**Hook**: When Mom says bedtime, the living room becomes a kingdom.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A blocky living room. Evening. Mom's voice from off-screen: "Bedtime!" Two cube siblings — brother and sister — exchange a look. The sister's face drops. The brother's eyes light up. He grabs the first couch cushion. The sister catches on. A silent alliance is formed.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: A time-lapse of the siblings transforming the living room. Couch cushions become walls. Blankets draped over chairs become a roof. A broom becomes a flagpole. Pillows stacked into towers. String lights strung from corner to corner. The siblings high-five. They crawl inside. The camera follows them in — the interior is impossibly large, a pixel castle interior with stone walls, a drawbridge, and a flag flying high.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: Mom enters the living room. She was about to say "I said bedtime" — but she stops. She sees the fort. She crouches down. Peers through the blanket entrance. Inside, the siblings are on pillow thrones, arms crossed, expressions defiant. The sister holds up a paper crown. Mom looks at the fort. Looks at the siblings. Looks at the clock. Looks back at the fort. She crawls inside.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: Mom is inside the fort. The siblings are on either side of her. The sister has placed the paper crown on Mom's head. Mom is pointing at a "window" in the blanket wall. The brother is explaining the kingdom. The camera pulls back — the fort glows warmly from the inside. Voices murmur. The clock on the wall shows it's way past bedtime. The living room is a mess. But the laughter floats out of the fort. Subtitle: "Kingdom."

**Music**: 8-bit adventurous build → royal fanfare → soft acoustic guitar lullaby

## 2.5 — The Monster Check (Parent-Child Ritual)

**Hook**: Every night, the same ritual. Dad checks under the bed. The closet. The window. Nothing is ever there. But the ritual matters.

**Beat 1 (0-7s)**: A dark blocky bedroom. A cube-shaped child is in bed, blanket pulled up to the chin. A blocky dad is sitting on the edge of the bed. The child points at the closet. The closet door is closed. The child's eyes are wide. Dad sighs. Stands up. Walks to the closet. Opens the door. Shows the child the inside — just clothes. Nothing else. Closes the door. The child exhales.

**Beat 2 (7-16s)**: The child points under the bed. Dad gets down on hands and knees. Peers under. Reaches under. Pulls out a lost sock. Holds it up. The child shakes their head. Dad reaches under again. Pulls out a dust bunny. Holds it up. The child shakes their head again. Dad sighs dramatically — the sigh of a dad who has done this every night for three years. Dad reaches under one more time. Pauses. His expression changes. He pulls his hand back slowly. The child's eyes go wide. Dad's hand emerges — holding a toy dinosaur. The child laughs. Dad hands over the dinosaur. The child hugs it.

**Beat 3 (16-24s)**: Dad stands up. Walks to the window. Checks the lock. Pulls the curtains closed. Walks to the door. Pauses. Turns back. The child is watching him. Dad walks back to the bed. Sits down again. The child looks at him. Dad reaches out and ruffles the child's hair. The child's eyes are getting heavy. Dad stands up again. Walks to the door. The child is already half asleep.

**Beat 4 (24-30s)**: Dad turns off the light. The nightlight glows. Dad stands in the doorway, looking at the child. The child is asleep, clutching the toy dinosaur. Dad's face softens. He closes the door gently. The camera stays in the room. The child is peaceful. The closet is closed. The space under the bed is empty. The window is locked. The nightlight glows. Subtitle: "Every night. No exceptions."

**Music**: 8-bit music box → gentle dad-theme piano → soft wind chime resolution

## Growing Up Stories — Common Elements

- **Family-centered**: Every story involves at least one family member — sibling, mom, or dad
- **The love is in the actions, not the words**: Minimal dialogue — the emotional weight is carried by gestures
- **Conflict resolves through small gestures**: A shared cookie, a revealed scar, a pillow fort, a nightly ritual
- **Parents are gentle, not angry**: When parents intervene, they meet the child where they are
- **Warm, cozy visual palette**: Evening/nighttime settings, warm artificial light, soft shadows
- **Subtitles**: Short, warm, often one word (Siblings. / Kingdom. / Midnight alliance.)
- **Music**: Starts with tension or resistance, resolves to warm, acoustic, gentle
- **The ending is a quiet moment**: Not a big celebration — just a shared glance, a touch, a silence
